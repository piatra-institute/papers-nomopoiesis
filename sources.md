## Bibliography

Aubin, J.-P. (1991). *Viability Theory*. Boston: Birkhäuser. — verified (Systems & Control: Foundations & Applications; ISBN 978-0-8176-3571-8); origin text for the viability kernel. Supports the niche as a controlled viability kernel.

Constant, A., Ramstead, M. J. D., Veissière, S. P. L., Campbell, J. O., & Friston, K. J. (2018). A variational approach to niche construction. *Journal of the Royal Society Interface*, 15(141), 20170685. — verified (DOI 10.1098/rsif.2017.0685; PMID 29643221); active-inference lens, cited as one formulation.

Dawkins, R. (1982). *The Extended Phenotype: The Gene as the Unit of Selection*. Oxford: W. H. Freeman. — verified; 1982 first edition subtitle and publisher (the "Long Reach of the Gene" subtitle and OUP belong to later editions). Supports the extended-phenotype reading.

Geritz, S. A. H., Kisdi, É., Meszéna, G., & Metz, J. A. J. (1998). Evolutionarily singular strategies and the adaptive growth and branching of the evolutionary tree. *Evolutionary Ecology*, 12(1), 35--57. — verified (DOI 10.1023/A:1006554906681); the selection gradient as the derivative of invasion fitness.

Ghani, N., Hedges, J., Winschel, V., & Zahn, P. (2018). Compositional game theory. In *Proceedings of the 33rd Annual ACM/IEEE Symposium on Logic in Computer Science*, 472--481. — verified (DOI 10.1145/3209108.3209165; arXiv:1603.04641); open games / lenses / symmetric monoidal composition.

Gross, T., & Blasius, B. (2008). Adaptive coevolutionary networks: a review. *Journal of the Royal Society Interface*, 5(20), 259--271. — verified (DOI 10.1098/rsif.2007.1229); coevolution of node state and topology.

Kerferd, G. B. (1981). *The Sophistic Movement*. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press. — verified (ISBN 0-521-28357-4); dedicated treatment of the nomos-physis controversy. Supports the physis/nomos framing.

Laio, A., & Parrinello, M. (2002). Escaping free-energy minima. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 99(20), 12562--12566. — verified (DOI 10.1073/pnas.202427399); the founding metadynamics paper. Cited only to flag the "metadynamics" name collision. (Replaces a hallucinated seed citation whose title and DOI belonged to two different papers.)

Laland, K. N., Odling-Smee, F. J., & Feldman, M. W. (1999). Evolutionary consequences of niche construction and their implications for ecology. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 96(18), 10242--10247. — verified (DOI 10.1073/pnas.96.18.10242; PMID 10468593); niche construction generating selection and ecological inheritance.

Lee, E. D., Flack, J. C., & Krakauer, D. C. (2022). Outsourcing memory through niche construction. *arXiv preprint* arXiv:2209.00476. — verified (also bioRxiv 10.1101/2022.09.01.506204); a 2022 preprint, distinct from the authors' 2024 *Proc. R. Soc. B* paper. Supports the niche as writable external memory.

Lehmann, L. (2008). The adaptive dynamics of niche constructing traits in spatially subdivided populations: evolving posthumous extended phenotypes. *Evolution*, 62(3), 549--566. — verified (DOI 10.1111/j.1558-5646.2007.00291.x); niche-mediated selection and delayed benefits to descendants. Supports the niche-response kernel and the association factor.

Maturana, H. R., & Varela, F. J. (1980). *Autopoiesis and Cognition: The Realization of the Living*. Dordrecht: D. Reidel. — verified (Boston Studies in the Philosophy of Science, vol. 42); the defining text of autopoiesis. Cited to distinguish nomopoiesis from autopoiesis.

Metz, J. A. J., Nisbet, R. M., & Geritz, S. A. H. (1992). How should we define "fitness" for general ecological scenarios? *Trends in Ecology & Evolution*, 7(6), 198--202. — verified (DOI 10.1016/0169-5347(92)90073-K; PMID 21236007); invasion growth rate as fitness. Supports the guardrail that fitness is lineage growth, not utility.

Moulay, E., & Baguelin, M. (2005). Meta-dynamical adaptive systems and their applications to a fractal algorithm and a biological model. *Physica D: Nonlinear Phenomena*, 207(1--2), 79--90. — verified (DOI 10.1016/j.physd.2005.05.013); defines a "meta-dynamical adaptive system." The nearest existing prior term. (Cite the 2005 journal version, not the 2007 arXiv post-print nlin/0703026.)

Odling-Smee, F. J., Laland, K. N., & Feldman, M. W. (2003). *Niche Construction: The Neglected Process in Evolution*. Princeton, NJ: Princeton University Press. — verified (Monographs in Population Biology 37; ISBN 0-691-04437-6); the book-length statement, ecological inheritance as a second inheritance system.

Su, Q., McAvoy, A., Wang, L., & Nowak, M. A. (2019). Evolutionary dynamics with game transitions. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 116(51), 25398--25404. — verified (DOI 10.1073/pnas.1908936116; PMID 31772008); behaviors plus the current game determine the next game. Supports the strong reading in which play changes which game exists.

Todorov, E. (2009). Efficient computation of optimal actions. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 106(28), 11478--11483. — verified (DOI 10.1073/pnas.0710743106); optimal control cost as a KL divergence from the passive path distribution.

Weitz, J. S., Eksin, C., Paarporn, K., Brown, S. P., & Ratcliff, W. C. (2016). An oscillating tragedy of the commons in replicator dynamics with game-environment feedback. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 113(47), E7518--E7525. — verified (DOI 10.1073/pnas.1604096113; PMC5127343); game-environment feedback producing sustained oscillation. Supports simulation demonstration 4.

Wilson, K. G. (1975). The renormalization group: critical phenomena and the Kondo problem. *Reviews of Modern Physics*, 47(4), 773--840. — verified (DOI 10.1103/RevModPhys.47.773); the renormalization group. Supports the coarse-graining program.

Wong, M. L., Cleland, C. E., Arend, D., Bartlett, S., Cleaves, H. J., Demarest, H., Prabhu, A., Lunine, J. I., & Hazen, R. M. (2023). On the roles of function and selection in evolving systems. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 120(43), e2310223120. — verified (DOI 10.1073/pnas.2310223120); a proposed law of increasing functional information, combinatorial possibility expanding under selection. Supports generative nomopoiesis (phrased as functional-information/possibility-space expansion).
